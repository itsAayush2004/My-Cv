# Dialogue Toolkit: 18 devices with formulas

Each device has a **formula**, an **original example** (Hinglish) and a note on **when to use it**. Use these in Stage 5 and Stage 6 of the system.

---

## Character-driven devices (BB Ki Vines engine)

### 1. Deadpan bomb
**Formula:** serious or emotional moment → irrelevant, dark or selfish line in the **same flat tone** → no reaction from the speaker.
> **BABLOO:** Mama, mujhe lagta hai main life mein fail ho gaya.
> **MAMA:** *(reading newspaper)* Hmm. Waise aaj aloo ke parathe hai.

**Use:** to deflate drama. Best from the oldest or calmest character.

### 2. The taunt chain
**Formula:** simple question → verdict on your life → comparison → oddly specific detail.
> **BETA:** Mummy, ek kurta naya le lun?
> **MUMMY:** Naya? Pichla wala kis museum mein rakha hai? Gupta aunty ka beta toh apne kapde khud press karta hai. Collar tak.

### 3. Comeback ladder
**Formula:** insult → bigger insult → bigger → the first person gives up or wins with a **callback**.
> **A:** Tera phone itna purana hai, ismein snake game update aata hai.
> **B:** Tera wala itna slow hai, notification aata hai toh pichle saal ka.
> **A:** Kam se kam mera charger mera hai.
> **B:** *(silent. Cut. He quietly unplugs A's charger.)*

### 4. Literal misread
**Formula:** idiom or instruction → the naive character executes it literally → cut to the result.
> **BHAI:** Mummy aaye toh thoda nazar rakhna.
> *(cut: chota bhai is staring at the door with binoculars)*

### 5. Disproportionate escalation
**Formula:** tiny problem → loud character's reaction triples with every line → straight character tries and fails to stop it.
> **A:** Bhai, canteen wale ne samosa chhota diya.
> **B:** Chhota? Chal. Aaj ya toh samosa bada hoga ya canteen chhoti.

### 6. Verbal fingerprint
**Formula:** each character gets **one** signature word, sound or sentence shape, used at line ends as a rhythm closer.
- The uncle always says *"Dekho..."* before a terrible piece of advice.
- The loud friend ends threats with a place name: *"...Rohini tak chhod ke aaunga."*
- The mom answers questions with questions.

**Use:** at least once per scene, at most three times. It's a drumbeat, not a crutch.

## Situation-driven devices (Raj Grover engine)

### 7. Code-switch reveal
**Formula:** the English part is the **performance**, the Hindi part is the **truth**.
> **PAPA (on phone to boss):** Yes sir, absolutely sir, I'm on it sir. *(hangs up)* Kal subah tak bhool jaunga.

### 8. The verbatim line
**Formula:** the exact phrase every Indian parent, teacher or sibling says, unpolished → a cut that **contradicts** it.
> **MUMMY:** Main kisi ki baat nahi sunti, apne mann ki karti hoon.
> *(cut: she's on the phone, "Haan didi, aap bolo toh woh hi bana leti hoon.")*

### 9. Injustice mirror
**Formula:** the same action twice → two different reactions depending on who did it → identical camera framing both times.
> *(Chota spills milk)* **MUMMY:** Koi baat nahi, bachcha hai.
> *(Bada spills milk)* **MUMMY:** Bachcha hai kya? 17 saal ka ho gaya!

### 10. Heat dial (keep it consistent per script)
| Level | Description | Hard closer |
|---|---|---|
| **0, Clean** *(Raj)* | Family-safe, school-safe, brand-safe | SFX, chappal, freeze-frame, look to camera |
| **1, Mild** | "Pagal", "ullu", "chal hatt", light insults | Signature word, smash cut |
| **2, Street** *(BB-adjacent)* | Bleeped profanity as rhythm | `[BEEP]` as the closer, still on the last syllable |

**Rule:** the closer always lands on the **last beat** of the line, whatever the heat level.

### 11. Threat inflation
**Formula:** parent threat → the hyper-specific punishment that is never carried out.
> **PAPA:** Agli baar 90 se kam aaye na, toh tumhe mama ke gaon bhej dunga. Wahan Wi-Fi nahi, sirf bhains.

### 12. The kid truth bomb
**Formula:** guests or an authority present → the youngest says the private truth out loud.
> **RISHTEDAAR:** Waah, kitna saaf ghar hai!
> **CHOTU:** Haan, aapke aane se pehle 2 ghante sabne milke chhupaya.

### 13. Status flip
**Formula:** high-status setup (music, slow-mo, sunglasses) → hard cut → low-status reality in the same pose.
> *(slow-mo: he gets out of a car, adjusts his shades)*
> *(cut: the car leaves. It was an Uber. He picks up two sabzi bags.)*

### 14. Ages ladder
**Formula:** the same trigger at 5 / 10 / 15 / 20 / 25, one line each → the last line is a quiet, sad-funny truth.
> *Mummy says "Bahar mat jaana":*
> **5:** *(crying)* Mujhe park jaana hai!
> **15:** Bas 10 minute, promise.
> **20:** *(already out)* Haan mummy, ghar pe hi hoon.
> **25:** *(alone in a hostel)* ...Koi bolta hi nahi ab.

## Universal joke mechanics

### 15. Rule of three, break on three
> **MUMMY:** Padhai karo, khaana khao, aur... *(beat)* ...woh Sharma ji ka beta laptop dekhne aayega, room saaf karo.

### 16. Punchword last
❌ *"Roti bhi gol banata hai Sharma ji ka beta."*
✅ *"Sharma ji ka beta roti bhi banata hai. Gol."*

### 17. Callback
Plant an object or line in beat 1 and return it in the tag, now funnier.
> *Beat 1: Papa says "Iss ghar mein koi meri chappal nahi chhuta."*
> *Tag: the burglar runs off wearing Papa's chappal. Papa:* "Aur kuch le jaata, chalta..."

### 18. Written silence
In the script, these are lines too:
- `(BEAT)`: half-second pause before the punchword
- `(REACTION: dead stare, 2s)`: the cut where the audience laughs
- `(HOLD)`: stay on the face after the joke, don't cut away early

## Punch-up checklist (paste under every draft)

- [ ] First line is a question, accusation or demand
- [ ] No character answers a question directly when they could deflect
- [ ] Every punchline has its funniest word at the end
- [ ] At least 3 specific details (names, numbers, brands, places)
- [ ] At least 1 verbatim real-life line
- [ ] Every character's fingerprint appears 1 to 3 times
- [ ] At least 2 written silent-reaction beats
- [ ] Heat level is consistent
- [ ] One sincere or twist beat near the end
- [ ] A tag after the story ends
- [ ] 3 alt lines written for the top 3 punchlines
