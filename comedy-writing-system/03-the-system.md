# The System: from idea to shoot-ready script

This combines the two studies into one repeatable pipeline: **BB Ki Vines' character engine** and **Raj Grover's relatable-format engine**. Run every script through all eight stages. Each one has a gate you have to pass before moving on.

```
0 CAPTURE → 1 PREMISE → 2 MODE → 3 CAST → 4 BEATS → 5 DIALOGUE → 6 PUNCH-UP → 7 SHOOT SHEET → 8 SCORE
```

---

## Stage 0: Capture (every day, not just on writing days)

Bhuvan writes jokes down the moment they come; Raj mines his own house and school. Keep **one note** with three running lists:

| List | What goes in | Example entries |
|---|---|---|
| **Lines heard** | Exact words real people said. Don't fix the grammar. | "Main tumhari dushman hoon kya?" · "Itna toh hum bhi kama lete the tumhari umar mein" |
| **Situations** | Moments with built-in tension | Wi-Fi password changed by Papa · relative asks marks at a wedding · last day before vacation homework |
| **Traits** | One exaggerable trait of a real person | Uncle who forwards every message as "verified" · cousin who turns every chat into his gym routine |

**Gate:** at least **20 entries** in the bank before you start a script. Never write from an empty tank.

---

## Stage 1: Premise (one sentence)

Fill this in:

> **When [CHARACTER] wants [SMALL GOAL], [OBSTACLE] makes it [ESCALATION], until [TWIST].**

Example: *When Babloo wants to hide his result from Mummy, Titu Mama offers to "handle it", making the whole colony find out, until the result turns out to be fine and Mummy is angry about something else.*

**Gate, the 3-question test:**
1. **Recognition:** Would a viewer say "this happens in my house"? *(Raj test)*
2. **Character:** Does at least one character react in a way only *they* would? *(BB test)*
3. **Stakes:** Is the goal tiny to the world but huge to the character?

Two yes answers are enough to proceed. Three is a strong script.

---

## Stage 2: Pick the mode

| Mode | Length | Engine | Use when |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Vine-style story** *(BB)* | 4 to 10 min | Ensemble characters + plot | You have a premise with a plan that goes wrong |
| **B. Format sketch** *(Raj)* | 1 to 4 min | Container format | You have a list of recognisable types or a rivalry |
| **C. Short / Reel** *(both)* | 20 to 60 s | One joke, one twist | You have one killer line or one status flip |

Formats for Mode B and C: **Types of · X vs Y · The ___ Day · Different ages · Expectation vs Reality · Before/After · Transformation**. See `02-raj-grover-study.md` §3.

---

## Stage 3: Cast the scene

Use a fixed **ensemble** of archetypes. Make them *yours* by building each from a real person (Stage 0 traits list). Fill in a character sheet for each one (`templates/character-sheet.md`).

| Slot | Archetype | Their joke engine |
|---|---|---|
| **The Straight One** | You, the protagonist | Reacts, suffers, says what the audience thinks |
| **The Deadpan** | Uncle, grandpa, chill friend | Inappropriate calm, dark or irrelevant line at the worst moment |
| **The Naive** | Younger brother, simple friend | Literal readings, innocent truth bombs |
| **The Loud** | Aggressive friend, gym cousin | Disproportionate escalation, threatens first |
| **The Pressure** | Mom, dad, teacher | Taunts, comparisons, threats, selective hearing |
| **The Rival** | Neighbour, topper, sibling | Status games, one-upmanship |

**Cast rule:** every scene is **two slots in conflict**. Pressure vs Straight = taunt scene. Deadpan vs anyone = deadpan scene. Naive + a plan = disaster scene. Loud + small problem = escalation scene.

**Gate:** you can say each character's line *before* writing it, and it sounds different from every other character's.

---

## Stage 4: Beats

Use the matching template.

### Mode A: Vine-style story (6 to 8 beats)

| # | Beat | % of runtime | Job |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Cold open** | 0 to 5% | Drop into conflict. First line is a question or an accusation. No "hello guys." |
| 2 | **The want** | 5 to 10% | Protagonist's tiny-but-huge goal is clear. |
| 3 | **Round 1** | 10 to 30% | First character visit. One engine (taunt or roast). |
| 4 | **Round 2** | 30 to 50% | Second character visit, a *different* engine (deadpan or literal). |
| 5 | **The plan breaks** | 50 to 70% | The Naive or the Loud makes it worse. |
| 6 | **Collision** | 70 to 85% | Everyone or everything meets. The Pressure catches them. |
| 7 | **The turn** | 85 to 95% | One sincere beat or a twist. 2 to 3 lines maximum. |
| 8 | **Tag** | last 5% | One callback or deadpan line after the story ends. |

### Mode B: Format sketch

| Format | Beat structure |
|---|---|
| **Types of** | Hook line → 4 to 7 types (most recognisable → wildest → "that's me") → tag type that breaks the pattern |
| **X vs Y** | Same situation ×3 rounds, X then Y each time → round 3 flips who wins → parents' verdict |
| **The ___ Day** | Night before → morning → the moment → aftermath → next-day tag |
| **Different ages** | Same trigger at 5 / 10 / 15 / 20 / 25 (or 50) → last age is the sad-funny truth |
| **Expectation vs Reality** | Expectation (slow-mo, music) → hard cut → reality (same framing, no music) ×3 |

### Mode C: Short / Reel (4 beats)

| Time | Beat |
|---|---|
| 0 to 2 s | **Hook**: the conflict line or a visual question. Must work on mute, so add a caption. |
| 2 to 15 s | **Setup**: the expected pattern |
| 15 to 35 s | **Turn**: break the pattern |
| last 3 to 5 s | **Tag**: second laugh, ideally loops back to the first frame |

**Gate:** every beat has a **reason to keep watching** (an open question) at its end.

---

## Stage 5: Write the dialogue (the 12 laws)

1. **One breath per line.** If an actor can't say it in one breath, split it. Solo shoots mean one line = one take.
2. **First line = conflict.** Open on a question, accusation or demand.
3. **Never answer directly.** Characters deflect, taunt, change the topic or answer a different question. Direct answers kill scenes.
4. **Verbatim beats clever.** Use the exact phrase real people use from your Stage 0 list. Don't polish it.
5. **Punchword last.** The funniest word goes at the **end** of the line. "Roti bhi gol," not "gol roti bhi."
6. **Hindi for truth, English for pretending.** Switch language to show status or a lie (toolkit §7).
7. **Each character has a verbal fingerprint:** a signature word, a rhythm or a sentence shape. Write it on their sheet and use it at least once per scene. Never more than three times.
8. **Write the silence.** Mark `(BEAT)`, `(REACTION: silent stare)` and `(HOLD 2s)` in the script. In solo comedy the reaction shot is half the joke.
9. **Rule of three, break on three.** Two normal items set a pattern; the third breaks it.
10. **Specific beats general.** "Bina namak ki daal" > "bad food." Numbers, brands, places and names are funnier than categories.
11. **Escalate, don't repeat.** If a joke type comes back, it must be bigger, weirder or flipped.
12. **Heat level stays consistent** within one script (toolkit §10).

---

## Stage 6: Punch-up (three passes)

Run three separate passes over the draft. Don't try to do all of them at once.

**Pass 1, Cut.** Delete every line that doesn't (a) get a laugh, (b) set up a laugh or (c) move the plot. Goal: cut **20 to 30%** of the draft.

**Pass 2, Sharpen.** For every remaining joke line:
- Is the punchword at the end?
- Can a general word become a specific one?
- Can two lines become one?
- Would *this* character say it, or is it the writer talking?

**Pass 3, Add.** Look for:
- **Callback slots:** something from beat 1 that can return in beat 8.
- **Reaction slots:** places where a silent cut is funnier than another line.
- **Alt lines:** write **3 alternatives** for every key punchline and pick the best on shoot day.

---

## Stage 7: Shoot sheet (for solo multi-character filming)

Since one person plays everyone (like BB and Raj), convert the script into a **per-character shot list**. That way you film all of Mummy's lines in one costume, then all of Papa's, and so on.

1. Number every line in the script (L1, L2, ...).
2. Group the lines by character.
3. For each line, note: **eyeline** (where the other character "is", left or right of the lens), **energy** (1 to 5) and **reaction shots needed**.
4. Shoot every character's lines **plus silent reactions** (3 s of a stare, eye-roll, shock) for every line they hear.
5. Keep the eyeline consistent: if Mummy looks camera-left at Babloo, Babloo looks camera-right at Mummy.

Template: `templates/shoot-sheet.md`.

**Costume shorthand:** one prop per character, readable in 0.5 s (dupatta, specs, cap, gamcha, tie, moustache).

---

## Stage 8: Score before you shoot

Score each from 1 to 5. **Don't shoot anything under 28/40.** Rewrite the weakest category first.

| # | Criterion | 1 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Hook** (first 3 s) | Slow setup | Instant conflict, works on mute |
| 2 | **Recognition** | Niche | "Literally my house," people will tag someone |
| 3 | **Character voice** | Lines interchangeable | Cover the names and you still know who's talking |
| 4 | **Laugh density** | One joke a minute | A laugh or smile every 5 to 8 s (shorts) or 15 to 20 s (long-form) |
| 5 | **Escalation** | Flat | Each beat bigger than the last |
| 6 | **Specificity** | Generic words | Names, numbers, objects, verbatim phrases |
| 7 | **Turn/twist** | Predictable end | Surprise that feels inevitable afterwards |
| 8 | **Tag/rewatch** | Ends on the story | Final laugh, callback or loop |

---

## Quick start: one script in 30 minutes

1. Pick 1 situation and 1 verbatim line from your Stage 0 bank. *(2 min)*
2. Write the premise sentence and run the 3-question test. *(3 min)*
3. Choose the mode and its beat template. *(1 min)*
4. Cast two conflicting slots per scene. *(2 min)*
5. Write a fast first draft with no editing. *(10 min)*
6. Do the three punch-up passes. *(8 min)*
7. Score it. If it's under 28, fix the lowest category. *(4 min)*
