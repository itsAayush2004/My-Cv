| | BB Ki Vines | Raj Grover |

**Main lesson:** Raj Grover's dialogue is about **accuracy, not cleverness**. The laugh comes from hearing the exact line *your* mom says, word for word.

## 3. His format library *(observed from titles)*

These formats repeat because each is a **container** you can refill forever.

### 3.1 "Types of ___"
A list of 4 to 7 recognisable types, each a 5 to 15 second vignette with one joke. The order matters: start with the most recognisable, put the wildest in the middle, end with the most relatable or "that's me."
*Types of kids in summer vacation · Types of students on exam day · Types of relatives at a wedding*

### 3.2 "Chota Bhai vs Bada Bhai" (the rivalry)
Two characters, one situation, **opposite reactions or unequal treatment**. Usually the parents favour one side (the younger gets away with it, the elder gets blamed).
*Who gets the TV remote · Who gets scolded for the same mistake · Who gets new clothes*

### 3.3 "The ___ Day" (the event)
One high-stakes day told in chronological beats: night before → morning → the moment → aftermath.
*Result Day · First day of school · Parent–teacher meeting*

### 3.4 "Life at different ages" / "Then vs Now"
The same situation replayed at ages 5, 10, 15, 20 and 25. Each age is one line that shows how the reaction changes. The final age is often the twist or the sad-funny truth.

### 3.5 The transformation (status flip)
Set up a high-status image, then reveal the low-status reality in one cut. *"Cool guy in a car" → "sabzi wala on a scooter."* Expectation vs reality in its purest form.

### 3.6 "Before the test / After the test"
The same characters before and after an event, with opposite energy. A fast-talking, confident teacher before; panic and excuses the next day.

## 4. Dialogue techniques (with original illustrations)

### 4.1 The verbatim line
Find the line that **every** Indian parent, teacher or sibling actually says, and write it **exactly**, with no punch-up. Accuracy is the joke.

> **MUMMY:** Phone rakh do, aankhein kharab ho jayengi.
> *(she goes back to her own phone, watching reels at full volume)*

The line is real. The joke is the **contradiction** the camera shows right after it.

### 4.2 The universal threat
Parents' threats are hyper-specific and never carried out. Writing them is a craft.

> **PAPA:** Ek aur baar bola na, toh seedha hostel. Wahan pe roti nahi milegi, sirf daal. Bina namak ki.

### 4.3 Sibling injustice
The same action gets two different responses depending on who did it. Write both versions back to back with the **same words**.

> *(Minku breaks a glass)*
> **MUMMY:** Arre koi baat nahi beta, lagi toh nahi?
> *(Raj breaks a glass)*
> **MUMMY:** Pata hai kitne ka tha? Tumhare papa ki pehli salary se aaya tha.

### 4.4 Fast-talk vs freeze
One character talks at double speed (the teacher, the mom on the phone). The other freezes. The contrast in **tempo** is the joke, so mark tempo in the script.

### 4.5 The innocent kid line
A younger character says the true thing nobody is allowed to say, usually in front of guests.

> **GUEST AUNTY:** Beta, mummy ne kuch banaya hai special?
> **MINKU:** Haan, aapke jaane ke baad wala khaana alag hai.

### 4.6 Clean closers
With no profanity, the hard closer comes from:
- a **sound effect** (slipper whoosh, record scratch, dramatic sting)
- a **freeze-frame with a caption**
- a **smash cut** to the consequence
- a **look to camera**
- a **chappal**, the universal Indian punchline

## 5. The system behind the consistency

Things he has said, and what they mean for your workflow:

- **"Discipline, not algorithms."** Post on a schedule. Write a **bank** of formats in advance so you never post from an empty tank.
- **Clean image = brand trust.** Clean writing isn't a limit. It's a business model, because you can be shown to parents and sponsors.
- **10 to 11 recurring characters.** Even in situation-first comedy, recurring faces (the younger brother, mom, the teacher) build a world that people come back to.
- **Full productions alone.** Like BB, the solo-shoot constraint shapes the writing. Keep each beat filmable by one person with simple costume changes (dupatta = mom, specs = teacher, cap = younger brother).
